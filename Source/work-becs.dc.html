The situation

Blood bank operations ran on paper forms and spreadsheets. Donor records, screening results, component inventory and issuance were each recorded, but in separate places and mostly after the event.

The brief asked for digitisation. Take what is on paper and put it on a screen.

The constraint we found

Process

The binding constraint

Compliance checkpoints were being recorded after the fact rather than enforced during the work. A screening result written up at the end of a shift documents that a check happened. It does not prevent a unit advancing before the check is complete.

Digitising that faithfully produces a faster version of the same exposure. The question was not how to record the work. It was whether the work could be specified tightly enough that the system could refuse to let it happen out of order.

What we considered and set aside

Digitise the forms

What was briefed. Delivers a searchable archive and changes nothing about the risk.

Buy an off-the-shelf blood bank system

Viable in principle. The available options assumed single-site operation and would not hold a multi-branch network under one inventory view.

Encode the compliance sequence

The path taken. Build the workflow so that ISBT 128 requirements are gates in the process rather than fields on a record.

What was built

01

Donor lifecycle management

Registration, history, eligibility and deferral tracking held as one record across branches, so a donor deferred at one site is deferred at all of them.

02

Automated screening workflow

Screening as an enforced sequence. A unit cannot progress to component processing until the required results exist and pass.

03

Component inventory, live

Stock by component type, group and expiry across the branch network, visible in one place.

04

Issuance and audit tracking

Every unit traceable from donor to recipient, with the audit trail generated by the work rather than assembled for an inspection.

05

Multi-branch control

One operating picture across sites, with branch-level permissions and a single inventory view above them.

What made the difference

Treating compliance as a property of the workflow rather than a report produced from it. That distinction is the difference between a system that documents what happened and one that constrains what can happen. In regulated operations it is the only version worth building.
