# KalTech — Services Page Copy

Two structural problems before the copy.

**The page is a menu on a site that says menus do not work.** Three lines named by activity, presented for the reader to choose between. The homepage opens by saying most companies commission a solution before the constraint has been identified. A reader who accepts that argument and then arrives here is asked to self-diagnose, which is the behaviour the argument warns against. The fix is not to remove the three lines. It is to route by the situation the reader is in rather than by the name of the activity.

**The page does not answer the question it exists to answer.** The homepage sells the thesis and the diagnostic page sells the finding. This page has to answer what a buyer asks once they believe both: can these people build it, and what protects me if they cannot. Scope discipline, code ownership, what happens on an overrun, where the engagement can end. None of that appears anywhere on the site. Section 3 below is the largest addition and, in my view, the reason this page will or will not convert.

Sections marked **[NEW]** do not exist today.

---

## 1. WHAT WE DO

**Eyebrow:** WHAT WE DO

**Headline:**

> Strategy and build, under one roof.

**Standfirst:**

> The same firm that scopes the work ships it. That is the whole of the accountability claim, and it is why there is no handover between a strategy team and a delivery team.

> Three lines run out of that arrangement. You are not expected to arrive knowing which one you need, because establishing that is what the first conversation is for.

*That second paragraph is the fix for the menu problem, and it costs two lines. Without it the page silently assumes a self-diagnosis the rest of the site argues against.*

---

### A change to the three columns

Each service currently carries **WHAT IT COVERS / WHAT AN ENGAGEMENT LOOKS LIKE / WHAT IT IS NOT**.

The first column restates the intro paragraph directly above it. For line 01 the paragraph says the work covers the diagnostic, architecture decisions, build-versus-buy calls and roadmap sequencing against payback, and the column then says the diagnostic, architecture and data model decisions, build-versus-buy, and roadmap sequencing against payback. The reader gets the same list twice, six centimetres apart.

Replace that column with **WHAT BRINGS PEOPLE HERE**. It costs nothing in layout, removes the repetition, and gives the reader a symptom to match themselves against instead of a taxonomy to choose from.

---

### 01 — AI and software consulting

**Intro:**

> We establish what should be built and what should not. This covers the diagnostic, architecture decisions, build-versus-buy calls, and the sequencing of a roadmap against payback rather than ambition. It ends in a written finding and a scope, not a deck.

**WHAT BRINGS PEOPLE HERE**
> A build has been proposed internally or quoted by a vendor, and nobody in the room can say with confidence whether it addresses the thing that is actually costing money.

**WHAT AN ENGAGEMENT LOOKS LIKE**
> Two to three weeks, senior-led throughout, ending in a written finding and a priced scope.

**WHAT IT IS NOT**
> A strategy deck, a vendor comparison exercise, or a discovery phase staffed by people who will not build the work.

---

### 02 — AI and software development

**Intro:**

> We build and ship production systems. Platforms, internal tooling, integrations, and applied AI where it earns its place. Senior engineers on the work throughout, with no junior tier absorbing the delivery.

**WHAT BRINGS PEOPLE HERE**
> The constraint is established and the case is settled, and what is needed now is a system that survives production rather than a prototype that survives a demo.

**WHAT AN ENGAGEMENT LOOKS LIKE**
> Scoped against a diagnostic finding and delivered in stages, typically [X] to [Y], with the existing operation live throughout and a working system in production before the final stage.

**WHAT IT IS NOT**
> Staff augmentation, offshore capacity, or a prototype delivered with an invoice for the learning.

*The duration is the gap. Line 01 tells the reader it takes two to three weeks. Lines 02 and 03 give no shape at all, so a reader working out whether they can commit has to email you to find out, and most will not.*

---

### 03 — Technology strategy and transformation

**Intro:**

> We work alongside operators through a change that spans more than one system. Migration off legacy platforms, consolidation of fragmented data, and the operating decisions that come with both.

**WHAT BRINGS PEOPLE HERE**
> A change is already underway, it touches more than one system, and the decisions are arriving faster than a fixed scope can absorb them.

**WHAT AN ENGAGEMENT LOOKS LIKE**
> A retainer, because the work outlasts a single build. Reviewed at [X] intervals against what it has moved, rather than renewed by default.

**WHAT IT IS NOT**
> An ongoing support contract, a managed service, or a seat on a steering committee.

*Retainers make mid-market buyers nervous for one reason, which is that they are hard to end. Saying it is reviewed rather than renewed by default removes most of that, and costs you nothing you would want to keep.*

## 3. THE ARRANGEMENT **[NEW]**

**Eyebrow:** THE ARRANGEMENT

**Headline:**

> Every stage has an exit.

**Body:**

> The sequence above is four stages, and the engagement can end at the close of any of them with something in your hands. After the diagnostic, a written finding. After the scope, a priced plan detailed enough that another firm could build against it if you would rather they did. After a build stage, a system running in production rather than a branch waiting for the next payment. There is no point in the sequence at which stopping leaves you holding nothing but a relationship.

**Three columns beneath:**

**SCOPE**
> Fixed and priced before a stage begins. Where we have misjudged the estimate, that is our cost to absorb rather than a change order to negotiate.

**CODE**
> Yours. Repositories, infrastructure and documentation transfer at the close of each stage, not at the end of the engagement.

**PEOPLE**
> The engineers named at scoping are the engineers on the work. Where that has to change, you hear it before it happens rather than afterwards.

*This is the most valuable section available to this page and the only one that converts the accountability claim into something a buyer can hold you to. Every firm on your prospect's shortlist says senior people and no handover. Almost none of them will put an overrun position and a per-stage code transfer in writing on a public page, because both cost real money in the cases where they bite.*

**This is also the section I cannot write on your behalf.** Each of the three is a commercial commitment, not a copy choice:

1. **Overruns.** Do you actually absorb a misjudged estimate, or do you negotiate? If you negotiate, cut the second sentence. Publishing a promise you renegotiate in practice is worse than publishing nothing, because the first client who tests it becomes a reference in the wrong direction.
2. **Code transfer.** Per stage, or at the end? Per stage is unusual and is worth saying loudly if it is true.
3. **Named engineers.** Confirm you can hold this given the capacity constraints the About page describes.

Whichever of the three you cannot commit to, drop the column rather than soften the language. Two hard commitments read better than three hedged ones.

## 5. FIT

**Eyebrow:** FIT

**Headline:**

> We are not the right firm for everyone.

### This works when

> You have a functioning business and a specific commercial problem.

> Someone with budget authority is in the room.

> The system will run in production, in front of customers or under audit.

> You want the people who scope the work to be the people who build it.

> **[NEW]** You would rather be told the scope is smaller than you budgeted than be told the budget is achievable.

### This is not for

> Teams looking for additional capacity rather than a partner.

> Work where the brief is fixed and the constraint is not open to question.

> **[NEW]** Procurement run as a lowest-bid comparison against a specification that is not open to revision.

> **[REPLACES]** Companies without an operation for the system to run inside, where any answer we gave would be a forecast rather than a finding.

**Remove:** "Companies deciding whether to invest in technology at all."

*This is the third page carrying that line, and it contradicts the homepage on all three. The homepage names as a qualifying condition a commitment to invest in technology without yet knowing where it should land. That is the same reader this line turns away. Kill it site-wide.*
